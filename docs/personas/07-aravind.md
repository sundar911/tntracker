## How Aravind consumes election information

Aravind's political awareness comes almost entirely from Instagram reels, YouTube shorts, and memes shared in his college hostel WhatsApp group. He follows creators like Madan Gowri and Tamil political satire pages. He's heard heated debates in the hostel common room but feels overwhelmed by the volume of claims and counter-claims. This is his first election and he's not sure how to evaluate candidates beyond party loyalty.

## What Aravind needs from the app

- Quick constituency lookup — he votes in Karur (his permanent address), not Trichy where he lives now
- Bite-sized candidate summaries — he won't read long paragraphs; bullet points and cards work best
- A way to compare candidates side-by-side without manually opening multiple pages
- Understand what each candidate has done or promised — not just their party affiliation
- Shareable content he can post in his hostel WhatsApp group to seem informed

## Key accessibility considerations

- Fast loading on unreliable college Wi-Fi and budget data plans
- Content should work in both portrait and landscape (he sometimes watches in landscape)
- Short, scannable content — his attention span for political reading is ~30 seconds per page
- Visual hierarchy that guides the eye: what matters most should be immediately visible
- Mobile-first card-based UI similar to what he's used to (Instagram, Swiggy, etc.)
